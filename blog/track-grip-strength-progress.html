How to Track Grip Strength Progress: Dead Hangs & Forearm Endurance

Ask most polers what stops them mid-combo and the honest answer is usually "my hands let go" — not a lack of core strength or shoulder stability, but grip fatigue arriving first and taking everything else down with it. This is part of our guide to tracking pole progress, and it picks up straight from the strength benchmarks post: this time, grip specifically — how to actually test it, and how to tell if it's improving.

Why grip needs its own tracking, separate from everything else

Grip strength doesn't scale with the rest of your body the way you'd expect. You can build real pulling strength, core control, and shoulder stability and still find your hands are the first thing to give out — because forearm and finger flexor endurance is a smaller, more specialized muscle group that adapts on its own timeline, slower than bigger muscles and separately from them.

This is also the benchmark most polers never actually measure. They'll notice "my hands feel stronger lately," which is real information, but it's not a number you can compare against last month. Two simple tests fix that: a passive dead hang, and an active forearm endurance check. Both take under two minutes and need nothing but a pole or a bar.

The two grip tests that actually matter

Passive grip

Dead Hang

Hang from the pole with straight arms and engaged (not shrugged-up) shoulders, and time how long you can hold on before your grip fully releases. This tests raw grip endurance under your own bodyweight, without any pulling or core demand added on top.

  • Beginner10–15 seconds, hands and forearms shaking noticeably by the end
  • Intermediate20–30 seconds, controlled the whole way through with active shoulders
  • Advanced45+ seconds, or a shorter hold repeated for multiple clean sets with short rest
Active grip

Forearm Endurance

Dead hangs test passive grip; this tests grip under active load, which is closer to what a climb, spin, or transition actually demands. Do a slow controlled pole climb (or repeated squeeze-and-release holds on the pole if a climb isn't available yet) and count how many clean reps you get before your grip noticeably degrades.

  • Beginner1 controlled climb rep, or 5–6 squeeze holds before grip starts slipping
  • Intermediate2–3 controlled climb reps back to back, or 10+ clean squeeze holds
  • Advanced4+ controlled climb reps with minimal rest, grip still fully engaged on the last one

Common mistakes that make grip tracking useless

  • 1
    Testing with chalk or grip aids, inconsistently. Grip enhancers genuinely extend hang time, which is great for training or performing, but it means an aided test and an unaided test aren't comparable. Pick one approach and keep it identical every time you retest.
  • 2
    Testing after a class or conditioning session. Fatigued grip gives you a number that reflects today's tiredness, not your real capacity. Test at the start of a session, on fresh hands.
  • 3
    Retesting too often. Grip endurance moves slowly. Weekly retests mostly measure hand skin condition and daily fatigue, not real adaptation — three to four weeks between tests gives a truer picture.
  • 4
    Ignoring skin and calluses as a variable. Sweaty, dry, or freshly-callused hands change your grip test results independent of actual strength. Note hand condition alongside your number so you're not comparing strength gains to a skin-day fluke.

Building the grip that shows up in these tests

If your numbers aren't moving, dead hangs and light forearm work two to three times a week — kept separate from pole class, not stacked right before or after it — is what actually shifts these benchmarks over time. The at-home pole conditioning routine includes grip work alongside the rest of your conditioning if you'd rather train it as part of a full session.

And if grip is limiting you in class right now, not just in testing, our guide to grip aids for beginners covers chalk, grip enhancers, and tack — useful for training and performing, just worth leaving out when you're specifically testing your unaided number.
